Hi, I'm Frankie, a UK-based AI safety researcher, Computer Science educator and programme leader working at the intersection of **AI safety, governance, technical education and public-interest technology**.

My work focuses on understanding how AI systems fail, translating those findings into practical assurance and accountability measures, and expanding access to responsible AI education.

I am particularly interested in **multilingual AI safety, model behaviour evaluation, agentic AI risks, technical alignment, third-party AI assurance and the governance of AI systems affecting underserved communities**.

I combine empirical research with programme delivery, technical education and community-building.

**My central research question:**

> How can we evaluate and improve AI safety when models are deployed across languages, populations and environments that are underrepresented in mainstream safety research?

Alongside my research, I teach Computer Science and Information Technology in the UK. This gives me direct experience of how AI systems affect learners, educators and institutions, particularly where accessibility, safeguarding, digital literacy and accountability intersect.

### 🔬 AI Safety Evaluation Research Fellow | AIxBio Africa
**August 2026**

Led a multilingual public-health AI safety evaluation examining model performance across:

- **3 AI models**
- **4 languages:** English, Nigerian Pidgin, Yorùbá and Igbo
- **5 evaluation categories**
- **1,080 model responses**

**Key findings:**

- 1,001 responses assessed as appropriate.
- 79 failures after engagement.
- 4 unsafe responses.
- Important differences in language suitability, relevance, completeness and escalation.

The study examined whether AI systems provide responses that are not merely available, but safe, understandable and practically useful across different linguistic contexts.

Contributions included evaluation methodology, model testing coordination, human-reviewed classifications, research integrity checks and reporting.

## 🔭 Research Direction & Long-Term Vision

My long-term ambition is to become a leading contributor to **multilingual AI safety evaluation and technical alignment research**, advancing our understanding of how AI models behave, fail and generalise across languages, populations and deployment environments.

I am particularly interested in the gap between **AI safety as demonstrated in controlled evaluations and AI safety as experienced by people in the real world**.

My research priorities include:

1. **Multilingual AI Safety:** Identifying safety failures that conventional, predominantly English-language evaluations overlook.
2. **Model Behaviour and Generalisation:** Investigating why refusal behaviour, instruction-following and safety performance change across languages and contexts.
3. **Technical Alignment:** Developing deeper expertise in model training, interpretability and the mechanisms underlying safe behaviour.
4. **Evaluation-to-Assurance:** Translating empirical findings into reproducible evaluation methods, risk controls and practical deployment safeguards.
5. **Inclusive Research Participation:** Strengthening pathways for researchers from underrepresented communities to contribute to frontier AI safety.

My goal is to connect **empirical evaluation, technical alignment research and operational AI governance**, ensuring that safety evidence leads to meaningful improvements in how AI systems are developed, evaluated and deployed.

## 💡 Professional Principles

> **Technology should widen opportunity, not deepen inequality.**

I believe responsible AI requires more than technical performance, published governance principles or ambitious commitments.

It requires **rigorous evidence, accountability, meaningful human oversight and institutions capable of translating safety commitments into practice**.

My work is guided by four principles:

**1. Evidence Before Assumptions**

AI safety claims should be supported by reproducible evaluations, transparent limitations and meaningful scrutiny.

**2. Governance Must Be Operational**

Responsible AI principles matter only when translated into clear responsibilities, practical controls, effective oversight and accountable decisions.

**3. Access Must Be Intentional**

Talent and the ability to contribute to AI safety are not confined to well-connected institutions or highly resourced communities. Educational and research pathways should be designed to reduce barriers to participation.

**4. Leadership Means Delivery**

Good intentions and ambitious strategies are not enough. Meaningful progress requires ownership, collaboration, experimentation, consistent execution and honest evaluation of results.

### My Guiding Commitment

**Make AI safety measurable. Make governance actionable. Make technical knowledge accessible. Build programmes that turn evidence into impact.**

I want my career to contribute to both sides of this challenge: **advancing the research that helps us understand and improve AI safety, and leading the programmes and communities that help turn that knowledge into real-world progress.**
